\section{Multivariate Regular Variation and Spectral CdMC}
\label{sec:mrv-spectral}

\subsection{MRV tail measure}

The most general first-order dependence extension is multivariate regular
variation. We use the standard definition.

\begin{definition}[Multivariate regular variation]
\label{def:mrv}
Let \(X\in\RR^N\) and \(\Eset\subset\RR^N\setminus\{0\}\) a cone. We say that
\(X\) is multivariate regularly varying (MRV) on \(\Eset\) with limit measure
\(\nu\) and tail scale \(\Hb\in\RV_{-\alpha}\), written
\(X\in\MRV(\alpha,\Hb,\nu,\Eset)\), if \(\nu\) is a non-zero Radon measure on
\(\Eset\) satisfying \(\nu(tA)=t^{-\alpha}\nu(A)\) for all \(t>0\) and Borel
\(A\subset\Eset\) bounded away from the origin, and
\[
  \frac{\Pb(X/x\in\cdot)}{\Hb(x)}\xrightarrow{v}\nu(\cdot)
  \quad\text{vaguely on }\Eset,
\]
i.e.\ \(\Pb(X/x\in A)/\Hb(x)\to\nu(A)\) for every Borel
\(A\subset\Eset\) bounded away from the origin with \(\nu(\partial A)=0\).
\end{definition}

In polar coordinates \(X=R\Theta\) the homogeneity of \(\nu\) factorizes the
limit measure as
\(\nu(\dd r,\dd\theta)=\alpha r^{-\alpha-1}\dd r\,S(\dd\theta)\), where the
angular measure \(S\) on the unit sphere or simplex encodes the directional
concentration of extreme vectors. The spectral measure is identifiable up to
the choice of radial normalization.

\begin{definition}[Spectral measure]
\label{def:spectral-measure}
The angular (spectral) measure $S$ associated to an MRV law is the unique
finite Radon measure on the unit sphere $\mathbb{S}^{N-1}$ (or on the unit
simplex when the cone is restricted to the positive orthant) satisfying the
polar decomposition above for the chosen radial normalization.
\end{definition}

For a linear loss \(\ell(z)=a^\top z\), define
\[
  A_\ell=\{z:\ell(z)>1\}.
\]

\begin{assumption}[MRV loss set is admissible]
\label{ass:mrv-loss}
The loss vector $X$ satisfies $X\in\MRV(\alpha,\Hb,\nu,\Eset)$ for some
$\alpha>0$, tail scale $\Hb\in\RV_{-\alpha}$, limit measure $\nu$, and cone
$\Eset\supseteq A_\ell$. The set $A_\ell=\{z:\ell(z)>1\}$ is bounded away from
the origin and $\nu(\partial A_\ell)=0$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[MRV linear-risk asymptotic]
\label{thm:mrv-linear-risk}
Under \cref{ass:mrv-loss},
\[
  \Pb\{\ell(X)>x\}
  \sim \Hb(x)\nu(A_\ell).
\]
If \(\nu\) has polar representation
\[
  \nu(\dd r,\dd\theta)=\alpha r^{-\alpha-1}\dd r\,S(\dd\theta),
\]
with \(r>0\) and angular measure \(S\), then
\[
  \nu(A_\ell)=\int (\ell(\theta)_+)^\alpha S(\dd\theta),
\]
under the chosen radial normalization.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Apply \cref{def:mrv} to $A_\ell$, then change to polar coordinates and
integrate radially. The full argument is in \cref{appx:dependent-proofs}.
\end{proof}

This theorem contains the independent leading constant as a special case. If
\(S\) is concentrated on coordinate axes \(e_i\), with masses proportional to
\(c_i\), and \(\ell(z)=\sum_i z_i\), then the integral collapses to
\(\sum_i c_i\). If \(S\) has mass away from the axes, joint extreme directions
contribute at first order.

\subsection{Spectral estimator}

Suppose we can simulate or resample from an angular distribution \(S/S(\Theta)\)
and evaluate the conditional radial survival. For \(X=R\Theta\),
\[\ell(X)>x \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R>x/\ell(\Theta),\]
when \(\ell(\Theta)>0\). The radial conditional estimator is
\[
  Z^{\mathrm{spec}}(x)=
  \Pb\{R>x/\ell(\Theta)\mid \Theta\}\1\{\ell(\Theta)>0\}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Exact radial conditional estimator]
\label{prop:radial-cdmc}
If the joint law admits a radial-angular representation with regular
conditional radial survival \(\Fb_R(r\mid\Theta)\), then
\[
  Z^{\mathrm{spec}}(x)=\Fb_R\{x/\ell(\Theta)\mid\Theta\}\1\{\ell(\Theta)>0\}
\]
is unbiased for \(\Pb\{\ell(X)>x\}\).
\end{proposition}

In an exact Pareto radial model with \(\Pb(R>r\mid\Theta)=r^{-\alpha}\) above a
threshold and with \(x/\ell(\Theta)\) above that threshold,
\[
  Z^{\mathrm{spec}}(x)=x^{-\alpha}(\ell(\Theta)_+)^\alpha.
\]
The relative variance is then independent of \(x\) and equals
\[
  \frac{\E[(\ell(\Theta)_+)^{2\alpha}]}
       {\E[(\ell(\Theta)_+)^{\alpha}]^2}-1,
\]
provided the numerator is finite.

\begin{proposition}[Spectral BRE]
\label{prop:spectral-bre}
Assume the conditional radial survival satisfies, uniformly over the angular
support used by the estimator,
\[
  \Fb_R\{x/y\mid\Theta\}
  \le K\Hb(x)y^\alpha, \qquad y=\ell(\Theta)_+,
\]
for large \(x\), and
\(0<\E[y^\alpha]\le \E[y^{2\alpha}]<\infty\). Then the radial estimator has
bounded relative error.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Estimating the spectral measure}

The empirical spectral measure is estimated from exceedances of a radial norm
\(\|X_t\|\) above a high threshold \(u\). For selected exceedances,
\[
  \widehat\Theta_t=X_t/\|X_t\|.
\]
The empirical spectral estimator of the first-order constant is
\[
  \widehat C_\ell(u)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{t:\|X_t\|>u}
  (\ell(\widehat\Theta_t)_+)^{\widehat\alpha},
\]
with threshold-stability and bootstrap bands. In the real-data section this is
reported for the FF3, FF5, FF5+momentum, industry-portfolio, and security-level
contribution vectors.

\begin{remark}[Identifiability of the spectral measure]
\label{rem:spectral-identifiability}
The angular measure $S$ is identifiable only up to the chosen radial
normalization (the norm $\|\cdot\|$ used to define $\Theta=X/\|X\|$ and the
radial threshold $u$). Different choices of norm produce different spectral
measures with the same first-order linear-risk constant $\nu(A_\ell)$ provided
the boundary continuity of \cref{ass:mrv-loss} holds. The empirical spectral
estimator $\widehat C_\ell(u)$ is consistent for $\nu(A_\ell)$ as $k\to\infty$,
$u\to\infty$, and $k/n\to 0$ at appropriate rates; convergence rates require
additional second-order regularity (see, e.g., \citet{EinmahlDeHaanZhou2016}).
In empirical work we report $\widehat C_\ell(u)$ over a threshold grid and
flag instability when no plateau is observed.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Tail equivalence in the MRV sense]
\label{def:mrv-tail-equiv}
Two non-negative random vectors $X,Y$ on a cone $\Eset$ are MRV-tail-equivalent
if there exists a common tail scale $\Hb\in\RV_{-\alpha}$ and a non-zero
Radon measure $\nu$ on $\Eset$ such that both
$X\in\MRV(\alpha,\Hb,\nu,\Eset)$ and $Y\in\MRV(\alpha,\Hb,\nu,\Eset)$ in the
sense of \cref{def:mrv}. When $X$ has $N$ scalar coordinates and the limit
measure $\nu$ is axis-concentrated with marginal weights $c_1,\ldots,c_N$,
MRV-tail-equivalence reduces to the univariate tail equivalence
$\Pb(X_i>x)\sim c_i\Gb(x)$ of \cref{ass:rv-common-ref}, recovering the
independent baseline.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Comparison with dependent-sum simulation}

MRV gives a first-order constant and a natural estimator when the vector itself
is the extreme object. It is complementary to the dependent-sum simulation
literature, which often constructs importance samplers by conditioning on one
large component or by transforming dependent structures such as log-elliptical
vectors. The spectral estimator is most attractive when empirical angular data
show stable non-axis mass and when a radial tail model is credible. If angular
mass is concentrated on a small number of rays, a latent-shock model may be more
parsimonious. If angular mass is concentrated on coordinate axes but pair cones
are non-negligible at a slower scale, hidden regular variation is the next
layer.
